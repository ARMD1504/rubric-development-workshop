AI-Assisted Self-Review and Learning Reflection

You have built your rubric. Now let us use an AI tool to check your work, and then reflect on what you have learned throughout this workshop.

Part 1: AI-Assisted Self-Review

You can use any AI chatbot (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or similar) to review your rubric. Copy the prompt below, paste it into your chosen AI tool, and include your rubric details. The AI will evaluate your rubric against the quality principles covered in this workshop (Kasneci et al., 2023).

AI tools can serve as a useful "second pair of eyes" when developing assessment instruments, identifying structural issues such as inconsistent parallel language or missing criteria that are easy to overlook in your own writing (Luckin et al., 2016). However, remember that AI feedback should supplement — not replace — your professional judgment as a disciplinary expert.

Initial Review Prompt

AI Review Prompt

Copy this prompt and paste it into any AI chatbot along with your rubric.

You are an assessment design expert specialising in higher education rubric development. I am going to share a rubric I have developed for a university course. Please review it against the following criteria and give me specific, actionable feedback:

1. Alignment: Are the criteria aligned with the stated CLO and its cognitive level (Bloom's taxonomy)?
2. Parallel structure: Are the descriptors parallel across performance levels — does each level describe the same dimension with increasing sophistication?
3. Coherence: Does each level stay focused on the same criteria throughout, without shifting dimensions?
4. Observable language: Is the language concrete and observable, or vague and evaluative? Can a marker picture what the student's work looks like at each level?
5. Validity: Does the rubric measure what the CLO intends, without introducing unrelated criteria?
6. Weightage: Does the weightage reflect the relative importance of each criterion?
7. Cut scores: Are the performance level boundaries clearly defined?
8. Lowest-level descriptions: Does the lowest level describe what IS present rather than only what is absent?

For each issue you find, explain what the problem is and suggest a specific revision. If a criterion is well-designed, acknowledge that too.

Here is my course information:
- Course: [your course name]
- CLO: [paste your CLO statement]
- Bloom's level: [e.g., C4 Analyse]
- Assessment task: [describe the task]

Here is my rubric:
[paste your rubric]

Post-Revision Review Prompt

After you have revised your rubric based on the AI's feedback, use this second prompt to check whether the issues have been resolved:

Follow-Up Review Prompt

Copy this prompt after revising your rubric.

I have revised my rubric based on your feedback. Here is the revised version:
[paste revised rubric]

Have the original issues been addressed? Are there any remaining concerns? Is this rubric now ready for use?

Tips for Using AI Feedback

Getting the Most from AI-Assisted Review

  • AI feedback is a starting point, not a final verdict — use your professional judgment as a disciplinary expert to evaluate the AI's suggestions.
  • If the AI flags an issue you disagree with, consider why — the AI might be missing context about your course, your students, or your discipline's conventions.
  • Focus on the structural feedback (parallel, coherent, valid) more than stylistic suggestions — the AI is strongest at detecting patterns and inconsistencies in rubric structure.
  • Run the review twice — once before revision and once after — to see if improvements are recognised and to catch any new issues introduced during revision.

What's Next?

Continuing Your Rubric Development Journey

Rubric development is an iterative skill. Here are ways to continue developing:

  • Apply the calibration process (Page 8) with your department colleagues to build shared scoring standards and improve inter-rater reliability.
  • Review your CLO rubrics at the end of each semester and initiate CQI actions where attainment falls below threshold — use rubric data to drive continuous improvement.
  • Share rubrics with students before the assessment so they understand expectations and can self-assess their work against the criteria before submission.
  • Revisit this workshop's quality principles whenever you design a new rubric — the principles of alignment, parallel structure, coherence, and observable language apply across all disciplines and assessment types.
