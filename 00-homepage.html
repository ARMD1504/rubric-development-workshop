Designing Rubrics That Work

A Practical Workshop for Higher Education

This self-paced workshop guides you through designing rubrics that align with your Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) and provide meaningful feedback to students. Framed around constructive alignment (Biggs & Tang, 2011), the workshop moves from understanding why rubrics matter to building your own.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this workshop, you will be able to:

  1. LO1: Explain how constructive alignment positions rubrics as a mechanism connecting CLOs, assessment tasks, and teaching activities
  2. LO2: Distinguish between assessment-level rubrics and outcome-level (CLO) rubrics and their respective purposes
  3. LO3: Apply principles of quality rubric design to construct criteria and performance-level descriptors
  4. LO4: Evaluate rubrics against technical quality standards for continuous, parallel, coherent, valid, and reliable assessment
  5. LO5: Reflect on personal rubric development practices and identify improvements to current assessment design

Workshop Structure

The workshop is organised into four sections across 10 pages. Work through them in order, as each section builds on the previous one.

1

Foundation (Pages 1–2)

Understand constructive alignment and the role of rubrics in connecting outcomes to assessment. You will explore where rubrics sit in the bigger picture of course design and learn to distinguish different rubric types.

2

Building Assessment Rubrics (Pages 3–5)

Learn rubric anatomy, build a rubric step-by-step, and write effective performance-level descriptors. This section covers criteria selection, scale design, and the art of describing quality at each level.

3

CLO Rubrics (Pages 6–7)

Move from task-level to outcome-level rubrics and set attainment thresholds. Learn how CLO rubrics differ from assessment rubrics and how to use them for programme-level quality assurance.

4

Practice & Capstone (Pages 8–10)

Calibrate your scoring with colleagues, build your own rubric from scratch, and use AI-assisted self-review to refine your work. This section brings everything together in a practical capstone activity.

Before You Begin

Download the Rubric Development Template before you begin. You will use it throughout the workshop and submit your completed version at the end.

Workshop Details

⏲

Duration

Approximately 6 hours, fully self-paced. You can pause and resume at any time. The workshop includes substantial reading, interactive activities, and a capstone project that requires dedicated time to complete.

🎓

Audience

Suitable for academics at all levels — whether you are new to rubric design or looking to refine your practice. No prior experience with rubric development is required.

📝

What You Will Do

Read through concept explanations and worked examples, complete self-check quizzes with instant feedback, analyse case studies to identify rubric strengths and weaknesses, practise calibration scoring, and build or revise your own rubric using the provided template. At the end, you will use AI-assisted self-review to evaluate your work and complete a structured learning reflection.

What to Expect

This workshop balances theory (constructive alignment, rubric types, quality principles) with practice (building rubrics, writing descriptors, calibrating scores). Each content page includes reading material and at least one interactive element. The final section requires you to apply everything you have learned in a capstone activity where you either build a new rubric from scratch or submit an existing rubric for AI-assisted review and revision. Plan to complete the workshop over one or two sessions rather than rushing through it — the reflection and capstone activities benefit from time to think.

References
