Track A: Build a New Rubric

Track A Task: Design an Analytic Rubric

Select one assessment task from a course you currently teach. Using the Rubric Development Template and the principles covered in this workshop, design an analytic rubric for that task. Your rubric should:

  • Include 3–5 criteria aligned to the CLO and task requirements
  • Have 4–6 performance levels with parallel, descriptive language
  • Apply appropriate weightage to each criterion
  • Demonstrate alignment between the CLO’s cognitive level and your criteria

Before You Start

Before you begin writing your rubric, make sure you have the following ready:

  1. Have your course outline with CLOs ready. You need the exact wording of the course learning outcomes to ensure your rubric aligns with what students are expected to achieve.
  2. Identify which CLO the assessment task measures. Each assessment task should map to at least one CLO. If the task measures multiple CLOs, decide which is the primary one for this rubric.
  3. Note the cognitive level of that CLO (Bloom's level). Recall from earlier pages that the cognitive level determines the type of criteria and descriptors you need. A C2 (Understand) CLO requires different descriptors than a C5 (Evaluate) CLO.
  4. Consider what knowledge, skills, and attributes the task requires. Think about the dimensions of performance that matter for this task — content knowledge, analytical skill, communication, creativity, or other attributes relevant to your discipline.

A Completed Example

To help you visualise what the completed template looks like, here is an abbreviated example from a Master's-level course:

Example: Methodology Critique Essay

Course: Research Methods (Master's level)

CLO: "Critically evaluate research methodologies and justify methodological choices" (C5 Evaluate)

Assessment: Methodology Critique Essay (30%)

Criteria and Weightage:

  • Identification of methodological issues (25%)
  • Quality of critical evaluation (35%)
  • Justification of alternative approaches (25%)
  • Academic writing and referencing (15%)

Abbreviated Descriptors for "Quality of Critical Evaluation" (35%):

Level Descriptor
Distinction (80–100) Evaluates methodological choices against epistemological assumptions; identifies paradigm-level strengths and weaknesses; proposes well-reasoned alternatives grounded in methodological literature
Merit (70–79) Compares methodological choices across studies; evaluates appropriateness of methods for research questions; identifies key limitations with supporting evidence
Good (60–69) Analyses methodological choices with reference to research design principles; identifies some strengths and weaknesses with partial justification
Satisfactory (45–59) Describes methodological features of studies; identifies surface-level issues (e.g., sample size) without evaluating their significance
Fail (0–44) Reports methodological details without analysis; does not distinguish between appropriate and inappropriate methodological choices

Notice how the descriptors maintain parallel structure — each level describes the same dimension (evaluation of methodological choices) with increasing sophistication. The language progresses from describing to identifying to analysing to comparing to evaluating, which mirrors the cognitive complexity expected at a C5 level (Brookhart, 2013; Nitko & Brookhart, 2011).

Self-Review Checklist

Before You Upload: Check Your Rubric

Whether you built a new rubric (Track A) or revised an existing one (Track B), review your final version against the following quality criteria:

  • ☐ Each criterion aligns with the CLO’s cognitive level
  • ☐ Descriptors are parallel across all performance levels
  • ☐ Language is concrete and observable (not vague/evaluative)
  • ☐ The rubric is coherent — same criteria addressed at every level
  • ☐ Weightage reflects the relative importance of each criterion
  • ☐ The lowest level describes what IS present, not just what is absent
  • ☐ A colleague unfamiliar with the rubric could use it to score consistently

If you can check every box, your rubric is ready to upload. If not, revisit the relevant workshop pages to refine your work.

Track B: Improve an Existing Rubric

Track B Task: AI-Assisted Rubric Review and Revision

Upload a rubric that you are currently using in one of your courses. An AI tool will evaluate it against the quality principles covered in this workshop. You will then revise the rubric based on the feedback received.

Step 1: Upload Your Existing Rubric

Choose a rubric that you have used for at least one semester. It does not need to be perfect — the purpose of this track is to improve what you already have using what you have learned.

Step 2: Get AI Feedback

Copy the prompt below and paste it into any AI chatbot (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or similar) along with your rubric. The prompt is designed to evaluate your rubric against the six quality principles from Page 3 and the descriptor-writing guidance from Page 5.

AI Review Prompt for Existing Rubric

Copy this prompt and paste it into any AI chatbot along with your existing rubric.

You are an assessment design expert specialising in higher education rubric development. I have an existing rubric that I use in my university course. I have recently completed a workshop on rubric development and I want you to review my rubric against the following quality principles:

1. Continuous: Are the transitions between performance levels smooth, with no abrupt shifts in expectations?
2. Parallel: Do the descriptors use parallel language structure across all levels — does each level describe the same dimension with increasing sophistication?
3. Coherent: Does each level stay focused on the same criteria throughout, without shifting dimensions?
4. Valid: Does the rubric measure what the CLO intends? Are all criteria aligned to the CLO’s cognitive level (Bloom’s taxonomy)?
5. Reliable: Is the language concrete and observable enough for different markers to score consistently? Are there vague or evaluative terms (e.g., “adequate,” “reasonable,” “good effort”) that would lead to inconsistent scoring?
6. Suitable weightage: Does the weightage reflect the relative importance of each criterion to the CLO?

Additionally, check:
- Does the lowest performance level describe what IS present in the student’s work, or only what is absent?
- Are the performance level boundaries (cut scores) clearly defined?
- Would a colleague unfamiliar with the course be able to use this rubric to score consistently?

For each issue you find, identify the specific criterion and level, explain the problem, and suggest a concrete revision. Also acknowledge what is working well. Prioritise your feedback: list the top 2–3 most impactful changes I should make first.

Here is my course information:
- Course: [your course name and level]
- CLO: [paste the CLO this rubric measures]
- Bloom’s level: [e.g., C4 Analyse]
- Assessment task: [describe the task]
- Current weightage: [e.g., 30% of final grade]

Here is my existing rubric:
[paste your rubric]

Step 3: Revise Your Rubric

Based on the AI feedback, revise your rubric. Focus on the top 2–3 issues identified. You do not need to rebuild the entire rubric — targeted revisions to the flagged criteria and descriptors are sufficient.

Tip: If the AI flags an issue you disagree with, consider why. The AI may be missing context about your course or discipline conventions. Use your professional judgment as the final arbiter — the AI is a tool to support your review, not replace it.

Step 4: Verify Your Revisions

After revising, use this follow-up prompt to check whether the original issues have been addressed:

Post-Revision Verification Prompt

Use this after you have revised your rubric.

I revised my rubric based on your feedback. Here are the changes I made:
[describe the changes you made]

Here is the revised rubric:
[paste revised rubric]

Have the original issues been addressed? Are there any remaining concerns? Is this rubric now ready for use with my students?
